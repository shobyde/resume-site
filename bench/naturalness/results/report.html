boaz · naturalness spike · not shipped

Can an autocomplete signal rewrite answers better than the panel already writes them?

2026-11-24 19:42 · boaz 0.8.5 · 16 library tests · 13 answers · blind judge

Verdict: no. The rewriter joins sentences correctly, keeps every fact, and the judge prefers the original in 4 of 4 rewritten cases (4.75/5 → 3.25/5 on naturalness). The mechanism is tested and kept out of the release.

1. What was built

A new library entry point, boaz/text, with no model file and no network: a bigram model over the host's own prose, plus the one rewrite it enables. It cannot add a fact; it can only choose among wording that carries the facts already present.

APIWhat it does
createNgramModel(texts)Counts transitions over the corpus. score() is mean log-probability per transition; rank() and choose() order candidates.
mergeClauses(a, b)Joins two label or copula sentences into one; refuses shapes it cannot parse.
mergePronounClause(a, b)Joins a pronoun-led sentence to the one before it, in participial or semicolon form.
reflowSentences(text, model)Walks an answer, joins where a pronoun allows it, chooses a form by score.
observedConnectives(model)Reports the connective the corpus prefers rather than inventing one.

Tests: 16 passing, including determinism, corpus-vs-invented ranking, punctuation as tokens, both join shapes, refusals for unparsed shapes, both reflow styles, and a word-preservation check. Run: npx vitest run test/ngram.test.ts.

2. Method

Corpus: 964 sentences, 3116 tokens, taken from the site's posts and the project cards' own copy. Cases: 13 questions answered by the real engine, unmodified. For each answer the harness generates rewrite candidates in both styles, discards any candidate that loses a number, a quoted string or a capitalised name, and keeps the best-scoring survivor. Flesch reading ease and the n-gram score are mechanical; the third measure is a blind judge (minimax-m3.1-flash), which sees both answers in randomised order.

Original scored 4.75/5, rewrite 3.25/5; preferred the original 4 times, the rewrite 0, ties 0. Reasons, verbatim:

Example. Before: “Hobnob: Meeting note-takers ship your conversations to someone else's server. It runs on Whisper (large-v3-turbo), Local GGUF LLMs, Live captions.” After: “… server, running on Whisper (large-v3-turbo), Local GGUF LLMs, Live captions.” The judge called the second a dangling modifier.

5. Does the model's taste track the judge?

Each answer was also scored alone (mean 3.46/5). The correlation between the n-gram score and judged naturalness is -0.438; Flesch against the judge is -0.158. With 13 cases neither is a stable estimate, and the first one's sign is the wrong way: the more corpus-like an answer scored, the less natural the judge found it. The model is measuring topical familiarity, not prose quality.

6. Decision

Do not ship the rewriter. Joining shortens the answer and keeps every fact, and still reads worse, because the panel's sentences already arrive with their own boundaries. The module stays in the spike tree, unshipped and tested, so the next version of this question starts from evidence instead of a hunch.

What would change the verdict: a corpus an order of magnitude larger and in the panel's register rather than the blog's; ranking the realisations the phrasing layer already generates for one question, instead of joining finished sentences; or a build-time judge ordering candidates, where the model must be consistent rather than right.
