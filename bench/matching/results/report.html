When the words are nearly right, match on purpose instead of by accident

Two mechanisms, measured on the site's own cards: a near-miss name is rewritten to the entity the visitor meant before routing, and a feature question is answered by quoting the clause of the card that carries the words, or declining when no clause does.

The name half works; the feature half does not yet. 7/8 near-miss names now resolve and answer. Of 8 feature questions, 4 find a clause, but only two of those clauses actually answer the question (“transcribe” and “rules”); two are on-point-looking matches that quote the wrong paragraph (“need a server”, “quizzes for kids”), and they are shown verbatim below so the difference is visible. Controls: 3/4 declined, with one denial misread.

How the two mechanisms work

StepRule
canonicaliseCompare each word span (1 to 3 words) with each entity name after removing spaces and punctuation. Accept when the phonetic key matches and the spelling is within one edit (two for long names), or when the span shares the first three letters and is within two edits. Rewrite every accepted span.
evidenceWith the entity resolved, split its card into sentences and score each against the question's content words (exact tokens only). The first sentence of a card is the problem the project rejects, so it is ranked down rather than quoted. A clause containing "no/not/never" is a denial, and answered as one.

What still fails, and why

CaseFailure
“does hobnob work offline?”The card says “on-device” and “local”, never “offline”. A synonym map for features is the missing piece, not a better matcher.
“does groundglass test generated interfaces?”The card says “conformance tests” and “a stream describes”; no sentence carries both question words above the floor.
“does boaz need a model file?”The card says “No model, no API key”. It is a denial, and the denial branch needs to fire on “no model” before the token score decides.
“does sho byde dot com have a blog?”Rewritten to “shobyde.com com”: the “dot com” span is consumed by the name and the trailing “com” is left behind.
“who built hobnob?”Not a typo case: the name is exact, and the wrong rule answers. That is routing, not matching.

Two false positives, named: “does readback need a server?” quotes a paragraph about model endpoints, and “does readback have quizzes for kids?” quotes one about approval gates. Both share a word with the question and neither answers it. A feature matcher needs the question's head noun, not any shared token.
