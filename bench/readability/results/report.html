boaz · readability approaches · not shipped

Three rewrites aimed at a reader, and a wider bench to test them on

Sentence splitting, answer-first ordering, and plain-word substitution, each fact-preserving by construction. Judged blind against the untouched answer. Then the handbook grows from 170 facts to 520, with customers, incidents, runbooks and integrations, and the question suite from 40 threads to 53.

The panel is already short-sentenced. Across 14 answers only one carries a sentence long enough to split; splitting it wins (4/5 → 5/5, preferred) and takes Flesch from 4 to 72 on that answer. Answer-first is a tie (5/5 both). Plain-word substitution now applies to nothing: its only candidates were labels, and rewriting those produced “X is service is …”, which the guard now blocks. The next lever is not rewriting.

1. What each approach does

ApproachRule
shortSentencesSplit a sentence over 22 words at a semicolon or at “, and / but / so / while”. No words change; only boundaries.
answerFirstFor “Project: why the problem exists. It runs on X.” emit “Project runs on X. <purpose>.”
plainWordsLexicon swaps only: utilise→use, prior to→before, is built with→uses, a number of→several, and label→sentence for short labels.
