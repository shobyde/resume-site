This Week in Agents: Restraint Ships

2026-11-20 · news · by Sho Byde

Google shipped A2UI 1.0

A2UI 1.0 is the first stable release of the protocol that lets an agent describe interactive screens rendered with an app's own native components, with SDKs for web, Android, iOS and agent-side Python, TypeScript, and Dart. The adoption list at announcement time included Atlassian, Bloomberg, and ByteDance. I wrote about the 0.9 healing parser last week; the thing that makes 1.0 land for me is that a stable target turns speculative protocol work into ordinary library work, since renderer authors can now build against a versioned spec instead of a moving blog post. The open question I would track is widget catalogs: the protocol only pays off if apps stop hand-writing every screen type, and nobody has shown that yet.

Mistral shipped Vibe and open-weighted Mistral Large 4

Mistral paired its Vibe desktop app with Mistral Large 4, a 1T open-weight model the company says leads open models on FrontierCode and Terminal-Bench, with the larger weights published rather than gated. The design choice I find interesting is in the app: Vibe runs local and remote agents in parallel and makes you review diffs, which puts the slow human step in the middle of a fast loop instead of bolting approval onto the end. My skeptic's note is that "leads open models" is the vendor's own benchmark framing, so treat the ranking as a claim, not a measurement. The substance is that a frontier-tier open-weight release now ships as a normal product launch, not a special event.

The community pruned Gemma 4 down to laptop size

A community pruning run took a Gemma 4 26B MoE down to 3.8B dense, keeping 92% agreement on a classification task while running 79 tok/s on a Mac from a 3.8GB file. The number to hold onto is the task-specific agreement: 92% means the pruning was validated on one workload, and general-purpose behavior after cutting 85% of the parameters is untested. That limitation is also why the technique matters. Pruning tuned to a specific task turns "does this model fit my machine" from a fixed constraint into something you can trade scope for, and the local-model ecosystem has quietly become the place where that trade gets exercised first.

The question these three leave open is the same one in different clothes: how small can the trusted part get, the spec, the diff review, the pruned weights, before it stops covering the rest.
