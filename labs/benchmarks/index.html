A thread knows what a sentence does not.

Twelve conversations, replayed turn by turn against the assistant on this page: 21 of 30 turns answered with memory, 17 of 30 without. Then eighteen suites, each measured before and after the release that shipped it, and an LLM judge that reads the transcripts and says whether the answer actually answered. At the end, five approaches are run against a 170-fact handbook that is not this site, fitted, paraphrased and fresh.

Method, and what this does not measure

The two systems

boaz is the assistant in the panel: the rules engine, the site's knowledge, and a session that rewrites follow-ups ("and its stack?") into the question they mean. Stateless is the same engine, built fresh for every turn, with no session and no rewrite. That is the honest control, not a strawman: it is exactly what a single-turn demo does.

What a pass means

A turn passes when its expected facts appear, no excluded fact appears, the expected tool is the one called, and the answer is not a refusal. Refusals were the first thing the suite caught: a wrong route that ends in "I'm not sure" used to look like a pass.

The judge

Deterministic checks cannot tell whether a sentence answered the question, or whether it reads like writing. A model does that, offline, at build time: bench/threads/judge.py scores answered, tool_ok, grounded and readability per turn, and is given the question, the prior turns, the tools called and the answer. It never sees the expected facts.

The handbook arena

Five approaches answer 91 turns over a synthetic handbook (170 facts, 14 services, 5 teams, 8 policies, none of it from this site). The suite is fitted: it was written alongside the systems. Two more sets were written afterwards, one of paraphrases used to fix vocabulary and one fresh set that nothing was tuned against. The gap between the three bars is the honest result, and it is the same gap an LLM would not have.

What this does not measure

  • Nothing here runs a model in the browser. The assistant is the same deterministic engine; the judge is a build-time grader.
  • The eighteen suites are recorded, not re-run: each number was measured on the release that shipped that work, with its own tasks. They are not comparable as a single score, which is why they are shown as pairs.
  • Twelve threads is a small sample. The misses are listed rather than averaged away: "compare hobnob and readback" still opens one card, and "what can you do?" still declines.
